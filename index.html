HFGCS Monitor

The High Frequency Global Communications System (HFGCS) is the US Air Force's worldwide network of high-power HF stations used to talk to aircraft, ships and ground forces. It transmits unencrypted voice in upper sideband (USB), including coded Emergency Action Messages and "Skyking" broadcasts. Anyone can listen; the coded groups cannot be read by the public.

The waterfall below comes from the University of Twente WebSDR in Enschede, the Netherlands (0–29 MHz). It shows what is heard in Europe, not Kentucky.

Global Thunder 27

Global Thunder is U.S. Strategic Command's annual command and control exercise for the nation's nuclear forces. This page is temporary.

ExerciseGlobal Thunder 27 (GT27)
Run byU.S. Strategic Command (STRATCOM)
BeganAbout October 22, 2026 (most reports; one republished release is dated October 27)
LengthExpected to run about 10 days
Stated purposeTest and validate nuclear command, control and operational readiness across the strategic enterprise. STRATCOM says it is routine and annual, and is not a response to any nation or to current events
Visible activitySTRATCOM warned that bomber and other aircraft flights may increase. Airspace reservations were reported over the Atlantic, Pacific and Florida, including one Atlantic reservation naming Global Thunder, from the surface to about 25,000 ft
AlliesOne report describes British F-35Bs escorting a B-52 over the High North on October 24 in connection with the exercise
HistoryHeld annually; the first documented Global Thunder exercise was in 2014
Kentucky linkNone identified. No Kentucky unit was named in the public reporting we found.

What to watch for

Exercises like this can mean more bomber, tanker and command-post aircraft activity and more HFGCS traffic. The HFGCS tab and the Live Traffic tab show what is audible and visible; most strategic aircraft activity happens far from Kentucky.

The Emblem

The eagle is Wilma, a bald eagle from Lake Reba in Madison County. She is one of two eagles there, and she is missing a feather on her right wing, which the emblem keeps as her mark.

She is shown soaring, watching the sky, which is what this group does.

About / Rules

The 859 Military Observations Group compiles publicly available information only: ADS-B, official releases, news, public imagery, and observations shared voluntarily.

Rules: cite every source • mark confidence honestly • no personal info on service members or civilians • no real-time reporting that could endanger operations or personnel • no trespassing for "recon".

What this site tracks

  • Aircraft: military aircraft broadcasting ADS-B near Kentucky, with a rolling activity log around Fort Campbell, Fort Knox and Blue Grass Army Depot
  • Airspace: active FAA temporary flight restrictions in Kentucky
  • HF radio: the US Air Force HFGCS network, with a live waterfall
  • Installations: background on the major military sites, with each fact marked by how confident we are

Confidence labels

Confirmed directly observed or documented • Public record from official sources • Reported from unclassified documents or secondhand accounts

Limits

Many military aircraft, especially helicopters and special operations flights, do not broadcast ADS-B, so absence from the map does not mean absence of activity. Data lags by about 10 minutes.
